# Legal & Risk Notes — Squid Technologies game

Not legal advice. This is a working risk summary and a set of guardrails the project commits to. If this ever goes commercial (Steam/itch/App Store), get a 30-minute consult with an IP/media lawyer in your jurisdiction (you're UK-based).

---

## TL;DR
A **fictional company** ("Squid Technologies") satirizing **the whole big-tech genre** is low legal risk. The real exposure comes from (a) using a real trademark/logo, (b) naming or depicting a real person, or (c) implying official endorsement. We avoid all three.

The bigger practical risk is **not** losing a lawsuit — it's **platform takedowns and trademark complaints** that cost time and energy. The design choices below minimize both.

---

## Why satire of the genre is the safe play
Parody and commentary of an industry are broadly protected expression (US and UK both have strong traditions here — cf. *Silicon Valley*, *The Office*, countless games). Making it about "every big tech company" rather than one named target:
- removes the trademark hook,
- widens the audience (Google/Amazon/MS/TikTok refugees all laugh),
- and is genuinely funnier.

## Hard guardrails (this project follows all of these)
1. **No real company name** in the product. The company is "Squid Technologies." "Squid" is a Chinese-internet nickname riff, not a registered mark we're copying.
2. **No real logos or product art** — not Meta's infinity logo, not Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp/Workplace/Reality Labs marks, not anyone else's. The in-game mark is a generic rotated `∞` glyph we drew.
3. **No real people.** No CEO caricature, no named executives, no real faces. Bosses are abstract roles ("Calibration Council," "Half-Year Check-In").
4. **No claim of affiliation/endorsement.** The start screen states it is satire and the company is fictional. Never title it "Official ___" or "___ Employee Simulator."
5. **No defamation surface.** We don't assert that a real, identifiable entity or person did anything specific, illegal, or unethical. Jokes target generic corporate behavior, not factual claims about a real org.
6. **No confidential material.** Nothing in this game uses internal documents, real internal tool names that are non-public, real metrics, or anything that could be construed as leaking employer information. All terminology used is generic industry vocabulary ("calibration," "alignment," "scope," "stack ranking," "PRD").

## Things that would raise risk (avoid)
- Renaming the company back to a real one, or putting a real logo on it.
- A recognizable real executive as the final boss.
- Marketing it as "the Meta layoff simulator" with a real wordmark.
- Using a real internal codename, real internal screenshot, or real confidential data.

## Employment angle (separate from IP)
Independent of trademark law, there is a personal/employment dimension to publishing satire of an employer while employed there. This is a judgment call, not an IP issue:
- Keep it about the **industry**, not your specific team/manager/org.
- Avoid confidential info (already a guardrail above).
- Consider whether to ship under a pseudonym, and whether/when to publish relative to employment status.
- If unsure, this is the one place worth a quick chat with someone who knows your specific contract/situation.

## If it goes commercial — checklist
- [ ] Trademark search on "Squid Technologies" / final title in target storefront regions.
- [ ] Add an explicit "This is a work of satire. All companies, characters, and events are fictional." notice on the store page and title screen.
- [ ] Confirm no asset (font, icon, sound) carries a real third-party mark.
- [ ] 30-min IP/media lawyer consult (UK).
- [ ] Decide on author attribution (real name vs studio pseudonym).
